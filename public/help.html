Signing in

Sign in with your normal work username and password, the same ones you use to log in to Windows. Type just your username, without the domain.

You stay signed in for 8 hours. To sign out, use Sign out at the top right. If you're asked to sign in again partway through, your session has ended; sign in and carry on.

Searching

Type some words and press Enter or Search.

  • Results don't need every word. Documents containing more of your words come first, and a match in the title or file name counts for more than one in the text.
  • Exact phrases: put the words in double quotes, e.g. "annual leave policy", to find only documents with those words together in that order.
  • Browsing: leave the box empty and use filters (see below) to list everything that matches them, e.g. every spreadsheet from last March.

Choosing where to search

Search in: under the search box limits results to one source. All searches everything.

Keyword, semantic and hybrid search

The Mode switch under the search box changes how your words are matched:

ModeFindsBest for
KeywordDocuments containing your words.Names, reference and invoice numbers, file names, exact phrases.
SemanticDocuments closest in meaning to what you typed, even if they use different words.Questions in plain language, e.g. how do we handle a data breach, or when you don't know the exact terms.
HybridBoth, ranked together.Everyday searching: exact matches still rank well, and related documents turn up too.
Semantic search looks for what your words mean, so it can't follow instructions like "not PDFs" or "from 2019". Type only the topic, and use filters for file type, dates and the like.

In semantic mode every document is some distance from your search, so the number of results counts everything ranked, not just close matches. The order is what matters: the closest are at the top.

Reading results

Each result shows its title, source, file type and date, and a preview with your search words highlighted. If none of your words appear in the text, the preview shows its first few lines instead.

The date is when an email was sent, when a photo was taken, or otherwise when the file was last changed.

  • Expand shows the details (path, size, dates, and email or photo information where there is some) and the document's text, with your words highlighted. Very long documents show their first 1,000,000 characters; the rest is in the original.
  • Download fetches the original file. For email and SharePoint, buttons such as Open in Outlook open the item itself, and Open folder opens the folder it's in.
  • + Collection saves the result to a collection (see Collections).

Use Show to choose 10, 25, 50 or 100 results per page, and Previous / Next at the bottom to move between pages.

Narrowing results with filters

Click Filters under the search box, then + Add filter. Each filter row has three parts:

  1. What to filter on.
  2. is or is not. is not leaves out whatever matches, e.g. File type is not pdf.
  3. The value. File type and source are chosen from a list, which shows how many results each choice has. File type includes groups such as Documents or Images as well as single types. From, To, CC and Attachments match any part of the text you type, e.g. smith finds every address containing it.

With two or more is filters, choose ALL (AND) to require every one, or ANY (OR) for results matching at least one. is not filters always apply either way.

Example: Word documents or spreadsheets, but never PDFs: add File type is doc and File type is xls, choose ANY (OR), and add File type is not pdf.

Date range limits results to dates on or after the first date and on or before the second; you can fill in just one. Clear all filters removes them all. The number next to Filters shows how many are active.

Collections

A collection is your own list of documents, for example everything you've found for a project. Only you can see your collections.

Saving results

Click + Collection on a result. A small menu lists your collections, with a tick next to each one that already holds that result: tick or untick to add or remove it. To start a new collection with this result in it, type a name at the bottom and click Add.

Saving several results at once

Tick the box beside a result's title to select it. To select a run of results, tick the first, then hold Shift and tick the last. Select page ticks every result on the page. Your selection carries over as you move between pages and run other searches, and the bar above the results shows how many are selected.

Choose Add selected to collection, then pick a collection or type a name for a new one.

To save everything a search found, choose Add all results instead. It doesn't need anything selected, and it uses your current search and filters. If there are more results than a collection can hold, the best matches go in first.

The message afterwards says how many were added, and Undo takes back that addition.

Managing collections

Collections at the top of the page opens a panel listing your collections and how many documents each holds. From there you can:

  • open a collection by clicking its name;
  • create one, with an optional description of what it's for;
  • Edit a collection's name or description;
  • Delete a collection (this only deletes the list, never the documents).

On a wide screen you can keep the panel at the Left or Right of the window while you search, or at the Top. Your choice is remembered in this browser.

Seeing a collection on a timeline

Open a collection and choose Timeline in the bar above its documents. A chart shows when its documents are dated, and the list below runs oldest first. Choose how the chart looks; your choice is remembered in this browser:

  • Squares gives each period a square, with darker to brighter shades for more documents, and the legend shows the scale. Up to a year, it's one square per day, with a hexagon on the last day of each month to show where months end. Longer spans get a row per year with a square per month, or a row per decade with a square per year. Shades compare periods within the chart you're looking at, so hover over a square for its exact count.
  • Bars gives each day, month, year or decade a bar, which makes amounts easier to compare.

Click a square or bar, or drag across several, to zoom in: the chart and the list then cover just that period, in finer detail where possible. On a phone, tap to zoom; swiping scrolls. Stretches with no documents don't zoom. In the squares chart you can also click a decade or year at the left of a row, or a month name above a year of days, to open just that period.

While you're looking at one whole day, month, year or decade, buttons named after the periods either side of it, such as ← 1997 and 2001 →, move you along. They skip periods with no documents. Back returns to the view you zoomed in from, and Reset View returns to the whole timeline.

Each document's date is labelled Sent, Taken or Modified to show what it means. A modified date can be later than when a document was really written, and documents with no usable date are listed last.

Working on a board

Open a collection and choose Board in the bar above its documents. A board is a large canvas for laying out a collection's documents and how they relate, like an evidence board. Each collection has one, and it saves itself as you work.

  • Moving around: scroll to zoom. To pan, hold Space and drag, or drag with the middle mouse button; on a touch screen, drag with a finger. Fit brings everything into view.
  • Documents: the tray at the left lists the collection's documents that aren't on the board yet. Drag one onto the canvas, or use Place all to lay them out in date order. Taking a card off the board returns its document to the tray, and doesn't remove it from the collection.
  • Notes and other objects: add a Note, Person, Place, Org or Event from the toolbar, or double-click the canvas for a note. Click anything to edit it in the panel at the right, where a document also has its details, its text and space for your own notes.
  • Selecting: drag across the empty canvas to select an area, and hold Shift while clicking or dragging to add to a selection. The arrow keys nudge what's selected, and Delete removes it.
  • Groups: Group puts a labelled frame around what's selected, or adds an empty one. Drag a card into a frame to add it, or out to take it out. Moving a frame moves its cards, and collapsing one tidies them away, with their connections kept. Deleting a group keeps its cards.
  • Connections: drag from the small dot on a card or group to another one. Or choose Connect, then click one and then the other. Click a connection to give it a label, a colour or an arrow.
  • Timeline: the slider along the bottom shows how many cards fall on each date. Drag its two handles to choose a period, or use Play to watch the board build up over time. Cards outside the period fade or hide, along with their connections. Anything without a date always stays visible.

Suggest looks at the documents on the board and proposes connections and groups, with a reason for each. They're leads to check, not findings, and nothing changes until you accept it. Undo takes back what you accept.

  • Connections link an email to a file it had attached, emails in one thread (from their reply headers, or where those are missing, a shared subject and someone in common), documents with identical text, and documents that name a person, place, organisation or event on your board, all ticked ready to add. Files with the same name and documents with similar wording are also listed, unticked, as they're less certain. So are documents with a similar meaning, even in different words, with the two passages that matched most closely. Hover over one to see it on the board, or choose × so it isn't suggested again.
  • Group by offers topic, thread, folder, sender, period or file type, where they divide the cards usefully. A thread is one email conversation, from its reply headers. A topic's name is the words most common in it, which won't always capture what it's about. Each topic is labelled by how clearly its documents share wording: very alike (often a repeated template), clear, some shared wording, unclear, or too few to rate. It's only advice. Jumbled groups usually show as unclear, but so do some real topics, and a clear group isn't necessarily meaningful. All groups start ticked, and Untick unclear clears those in one go. Under a group, weak fits lists documents least like the rest of it, and you can leave those out of the frames. Once applied, click a group to see its label and why it has it. That's the rating from when the group was made, and it says so if cards have been added or removed since. Highlight weak fits marks those cards on the board, and Remove weak fits takes them out of the group and moves them below everything else, still on the board. Undo puts them back. Re-rate rates a group's cards as they are now, which is useful after moving cards, and Rate this group does the same for a group that has no rating. It reads the whole board, so it can take a little while on a big one. Re-shuffle cards closes the gaps in a group, oldest first, and fits the frame around its cards. Undo takes back either one. Applying one makes the frames and moves the cards into them. Cards already in a group stay where they are.

Text is only read from the start of each document, the first 20,000 characters, so something mentioned later on won't be found. A group's suggested title can come from the documents, such as their folder or common words, and like anything you type it stays on your board even if you later lose access to them.

A document's place on the timeline comes from its date in the search index. That isn't always when something happened, so you can set an Event date in the panel at the right, and it's used instead. Only you can see your board, and Undo takes back changes until you close it.

Your board saves itself as you work. To come back to it, open the collection and choose Board: it reopens where you left it. Save (or Ctrl+S) saves straight away, including where you've zoomed and panned to. Closing the board does the same.

Versions keeps named copies of the board, up to 30, to go back to. Save one before trying something out, such as a different grouping. Restore puts a version back, and keeps what it replaced as an automatic version. Automatic versions make way for new ones when the limit is reached; ones you named are never removed unless you delete them.

Reset board clears everything and starts again, after asking first. The documents stay in the collection and go back to the tray, and the old board is saved as a version called "Before reset".

If you lose access to a document, its card stays with your notes and connections but no longer shows anything about the document. If a document is removed from the collection, its card says so and offers to add it back.

Downloading a whole collection

Open a collection and choose Download all in the bar above its documents. You get one zip file, with the documents you can open now under their own file names; files with the same name are numbered. Where a file can't be included, because it couldn't be read or the download reached its size limit, a file called _not-included.txt in the zip lists it. Email and SharePoint items aren't files, so that list gives links to them instead.

Large collections take a while, and your browser can't show how far along the download is. You can run one at a time.

Finding more like a collection

Open a collection and choose a More like these option in the bar above its documents:

  • keyword finds documents that share the distinctive words of the documents in your collection.
  • semantic finds documents about the same things, even when they're worded differently. It also uses the collection's description, so a good description helps, especially while a collection holds only a few documents. A collection with just a description, and no documents yet, works too.

Both use the 20 documents you added most recently, and leave out documents already in the collection. Use Items to go back to the collection's own documents, and Back to search to return to your search.

A collection holds up to 1,000 documents, and you can have up to 100 collections.

What you can see

You only ever see documents you're allowed to open: the same people who can open a file, mailbox or site can find it here. That applies everywhere, including previews, collections, downloads and "more like these". If someone's access to a document is removed, it stops appearing for them, and drops out of their collections too (the collection says how many items are hidden).

New and changed documents, and changes to who can see them, aren't instant: the search index is updated on a schedule, usually every few hours.

If something isn't working

What you seeWhat to try
Nothing foundUse fewer or different words, remove filters, or check Search in is set to All. If you know a document exists, you may not have access to it, or it may be newer than the last index update.
"Some results could not be loaded"Part of the search failed, so the list may be incomplete. Try again in a moment.
Asked to sign in againYour session ended (after 8 hours, or when the service was restarted). Sign in again.
Download failsThe file may have been moved, deleted or had its permissions changed since it was indexed.
Download all says the collection is over the limitOne download has a size limit, and the message gives it. Split the documents into smaller collections, or download the largest ones on their own.
Download all says a download is already runningWait for your other download to finish, or cancel it in your browser, then try again. If the message says too many are running, try again shortly.
A board says it was changed in another windowThe same board is open somewhere else. Choose Reload to pick up the latest version. Changes made here since the last save are lost.
A board says it couldn't saveIt keeps trying while it's open, and Close warns you first if changes still aren't saved.
Files are missing from a zipOpen _not-included.txt in the zip to see which ones and why. Documents you can no longer open are left out without being listed.
A zip download stops partway, or the zip won't openThe connection probably dropped. Download it again.

For anything else, contact your IT team.
